# Position Sizing + Correlation-Aware Ensemble (2026-06-05)

**Ask:** make the existing edges more profitable via *how much we bet* — variable position
sizing (confidence-scaled, fractional-Kelly) and an "ensemble" that sizes up when edges agree.

**Bottom line:** **REJECTED — no sizing scheme improves risk-adjusted return over fixed $10.**
The "size up on agreement" idea is a statistical trap; confidence-scaling ≈ fixed; Kelly is
*worse*. The one thing that IS valuable — diversification across the 5 near-disjoint edges — is
already captured by running them as separate equal-fixed-size strategies. **No engine change, no
deploy, running strategies untouched.** Harness: `research/analysis/sizing_backtest.py`.

## Method (why it's honest)
pnl is exactly linear in stake, so total-$ is meaningless (any leverage inflates it). The
leverage-invariant question is whether a scheme puts more weight on *better* trades →
**Sharpe of the daily PnL**, plus an **equal-capital ($10 mean-stake) total-$** comparison (exact,
since linear). Bankroll walk applies the real caps: L2 capacity (≤0.8×depth), **hard_worstcase
$50 concurrent cap reserved-at-entry/released-at-settle** (the correct semantics — not cumulative
daily), and a per-window/direction macro cap for the ensemble. Fit on dev (05-23..27), holdout
(05-28..31), future (06-01..04). (Drawdown/Sortino were uninformative on 13 mostly-positive days;
Sharpe + equal-capital-$ are the basis.)

## Results — equal capital ($10 mean-stake), all splits

**Ensemble (5 edges pooled), eq-capital total $ / daily Sharpe:**

| scheme | dev $ | hold $ | fut $ | dev Sh | hold Sh | fut Sh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **fixed $10** | **867** | **511** | **586** | **1.35** | **1.00** | **1.61** |
| conf_ref15 | 865 | 447 | 547 | 1.26 | 0.96 | 1.21 |
| conf_ref25 | 867 | 515 | 561 | 1.34 | 0.96 | 1.34 |
| kelly_¼ | 646 | 192 | 615 | 1.25 | 0.88 | 1.17 |
| kelly_½ | 588 | 176 | 570 | 1.12 | 0.86 | 1.11 |

Fixed wins or ties on every split, on both equal-capital $ and Sharpe. Per-edge results are the
same story (fixed ≥ all schemes; Kelly worst). Confidence-scaling is ~neutral-to-slightly-worse;
Kelly degrades both because (a) it concentrates capital on high-WR/low-payoff trades (det_d12:
88% WR but win $3.13 / loss $10.21 → low per-$ edge) and (b) 13-day bucket-WR estimates are too
noisy (a ±10pp CI at ask≈0.85 swings the stake 0.5–3×).

**Why sizing can't help here:** the edges are *already* filtered on their confidence features
(distance, ask, time) — there is little residual within-edge quality variation left for sizing to
exploit. Sizing just adds leverage (more $ and more risk in equal proportion), not alpha.

## The ensemble "agreement" idea — a trap (pre-tested, confirmed)
Edges are near-disjoint (Jaccard 0.003–0.11; only 13.4% of windows have ≥2 edges). When ≥2
co-fire and agree, WR = **81.1% vs 80.4%** single-edge — **+0.7pp, pure noise** (n=127). Sizing up
on agreement would just over-lever the macro factor (daily-PnL ρ=0.22, det_d12↔fav_momentum 0.74;
effective independent bets ≈ 2.67 of 5; 69% within-window 4-coin co-move). So the ensemble was
reframed as correlation-aware capital *allocation* — but allocation tilts did not beat
equal-weight fixed either. **Ensemble engine meta-layer: GATED OUT (not built).**

## What this means (actionable)
- **Keep fixed equal-size ($10) across the validated edges.** That is the risk-adjusted optimum
  on this data. Diversification across the 5 edges (Sharpe ≈1.35) is the real free lunch and is
  already captured by running them as independent strategies.
- To make *more* money: add more genuinely-independent edges, or consciously accept more
  leverage/risk (uniformly scale all stakes — same Sharpe, proportionally more $ and drawdown) —
  **not** clever per-trade sizing, which the data shows adds nothing here.

## Decisions
- **No new strategies deployed. No engine change.** The pre-registered bar (beat fixed on
  risk-adjusted growth, sign-consistent across splits) was not met by any scheme.
- `det_d12_wide_v1` (round 1) remains the one new paper edge to forward-test.
