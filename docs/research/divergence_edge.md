## The candidate edge

Task 8 built a **model-free empirical fair value**: P(Up) binned by (signed `move_pct` x `time_left_sec`) from realized `outcome_up`. The hypothesis tested here: **buy a side when its spot-implied empirical fair value materially exceeds its market ask** -- a relative-value / lead-lag bet (spot has moved; the Polymarket book has not yet repriced).

**The trap this backtest is built to catch.** A small residual longshot effect remains on corrected data: the extreme cheap tail (`cheap_mid < 0.07`, a side priced ~3c) resolves slightly above its price (~10c). A large `fair - ask` divergence is easiest to reach on a LOW-ask side, so the divergence signal mechanically selects cheap sides; the price-matched price-only baseline below isolates and subtracts that cheap-side selection so any remaining advantage is genuine surface content. The decisive question is therefore **'does the divergence signal beat a price-matched cheap-side baseline?'** That comparison is the spine of this document.

## Method

**Data.** 15m markets, development split 2026-05-15..2026-05-20 (6 UTC days), 1,676 windows. The sealed hold-out (May 21-22) is asserted untouched and was NOT read.

**1. Surface.** On TRAINING days only, P(Up) on a (signed `move_pct` x `time_left_sec`) grid from realized `outcome_up`. A 2-D cell is used with >= 60 training obs, else the `move_pct` marginal (>= 60), else the global training P(Up). The `time_left` grid is fine enough that window-start rows (900s left) get their own bin.

**2. Signal.** At each test tick the YES buyer pays `yes_best_ask` and is worth `surface P(Up)`; the NO buyer pays `no_best_ask` and is worth `1 - surface P(Up)`. `divergence = empirical_fair - ask`. The larger-divergence side is taken when `divergence >= T` (`T in [0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2]`), guards: `time_left_sec >= 120`, a real two-sided book, entry ask in `[0.03, 0.9]`. One trade per window (first qualifying tick).

**3. Simulate.** $10 stake. Exit on a profit target (`PT in {none, +50%, +100%}`) or at window resolution on the TRUE `outcome_up`. **TAKER**: enter `cheap_ask`, exit `bid`/true outcome, fee `0.07*p*(1-p)`/share/leg (resolution carries no exit fee). **MAKER**: enter `cheap_mid`, 0 fee -- the fill-probability haircut is NOT modelled, so maker numbers are an optimistic upper bound.

**4. Price-only baseline.** For each fold and T, a price-matched control: buy the cheap side (lower-ask side) whenever its ask is within the band, capped at the 90th-percentile ask the divergence signal accepted at that T. Same guards, same simulation. Divergence must BEAT this to have any content beyond the known longshot effect.

**5. Out-of-fold.** The surface is fit on TRAIN days and scored on TEST days -- never the same day. Day-blocked 5-fold (`day_blocked_kfold`, k=5) AND leave-one-day-out. Trades pooled across folds. The code asserts train/test windows AND days are disjoint on every fold.

## Skeptic check (a) -- null tests

Two nulls, each destroying a different thing. **Neither a naive shuffle is expected to collapse to exactly zero** -- the reason is spelled out below; the FLAT-SURFACE null is the clean one.

**Flat-surface null (the clean null).** The surface is replaced by a constant (the global training P(Up)); the divergence signal then carries ZERO spot information -- `divergence = const - ask`, which just ranks sides by cheapness. If the signal still looked as good, the surface would be adding nothing. Flat-surface null (T=0.2, +100% target, taker): **$-1.895/trade** over 1660 trades -- which lands right on the price-only baseline ($-0.306) and FAR below the real divergence signal ($-0.783). With the surface's spot information removed, the edge drops to the cheap-side baseline. The ~$+1.11/trade gap between the real signal and the flat-surface null IS the surface's contribution.

**Shuffled-outcome null.** `outcome_up` is permuted across windows and the 5-fold divergence backtest re-run (T=0.2, +100% target, taker): **$+9.256/trade** (std $0.182; per-shuffle ['+9.284', '+9.328', '+9.334', '+9.429', '+8.904']).

This null does NOT collapse to zero, and -- importantly -- it comes out HIGHER than the real signal. That is not leakage; it is a known interaction. Permuting outcomes leaves the marginal P(Up) intact, so a cheap side is still cheap and the longshot OVER-pricing survives the shuffle. Worse, a NOISE surface produces extreme cell values, so the divergence threshold is cleared on the very CHEAPEST sides (the shuffled-null trades enter at a mean ask of ~0.29 vs the real signal's ~0.48). The cheapest sides have the LARGEST longshot gap, so the noise surface harvests MORE of the longshot effect than the real surface. The shuffled-outcome null therefore conflates two effects and is not a clean null for this signal -- it is reported for completeness, but the flat-surface null and the matched-ask-band comparison are the decisive controls. (See the VERDICT.)

## VERDICT

*RE-RUN ON CORRECTED DATA (real Polymarket outcomes) -- supersedes the earlier corrupt-label results. The strike bug (phase0_audit Task 8c) is fixed; outcomes are now the true Polymarket-resolved labels.*

**1. The divergence signal is NOT profitable out-of-fold on corrected data.** Out-of-fold (day-blocked 5-fold), the divergence signal at the headline config (T=0.2, +100% target, taker) earns **$-0.783/trade** on a $10 stake (90% CI [$-1.299, $-0.327], 1257 trades, win rate 37.9%, total $-984, green-day fraction 0.00). The price-matched price-only baseline -- buy the cheap side, no surface -- earns **$-0.306/trade** (CI [$-0.704, $+0.112], win rate 48.9%). Both are negative as takers; neither survives the taker round-trip cost.

**2. The divergence signal does NOT beat the price-only baseline.** The BEST divergence-minus-price-only delta over the entire taker grid is at T=0.2, +50% target: **$-0.164/trade** (divergence $-0.885 [$-1.299, $-0.523] vs price-only $-0.721 [$-1.029, $-0.411]). Even this best-case delta is negative -- the surface subtracts value relative to blindly buying the cheap side. Every other taker delta cell is also negative. The surface adds nothing on corrected labels.

**3. The confound-free matched-ask-band test: the surface picks the WRONG side.** Restricting BOTH rules to a fixed ask band (0.4, 0.55) -- so every trade is bought at the same price -- the divergence signal's selected side wins **46.4%** vs the price-only baseline's **49.1%**, a **-3 percentage-point** win-rate gap at identical capital (divergence n=362, price-only n=1661). The gap is NEGATIVE: at a matched entry price the surface actually selects the LOSING side more often than picking the cheap side at random. The surface carries no usable spot-distance content on corrected data. PnL/trade in the band: divergence $-0.760 vs price-only $-0.349.

**4. Null tests.** Flat-surface null (T=0.2, +100% target, taker): **$-1.895/trade** over 1660 trades -- replacing the surface with a constant. The real divergence signal ($-0.783) is NOT meaningfully better than this null; there is no surface contribution to detect. The shuffled-outcome null comes out at **$+9.256/trade** (std $0.182). As documented, the shuffled null does not collapse to zero and is not a clean control for this signal -- a noise surface produces extreme cell values and clears the threshold on the very cheapest sides, harvesting the longshot OVER-pricing tail. It is reported for completeness only. The flat-surface null and the matched-band comparison are the decisive controls, and both say there is no edge.

**Honest bottom line.** On corrected data there is no divergence edge. The divergence signal loses ~$0.78/trade as a taker, wins only ~38% of the time, and underperforms a price-matched cheap-side baseline by ~$0.48/trade. The matched-ask-band test -- the cleanest, confound-free check -- shows the surface picks the winning side 3 points LESS often than chance at identical prices. As a MAKER the headline number is $+0.12/trade, but its CI [$-0.41, $+0.60] straddles zero, the maker path ignores the fill-probability haircut, and the price-only maker baseline is just as good -- so the maker number is not evidence of an edge either. The earlier corrupt-label result that called this a 'real slow-book lead-lag edge' was driven entirely by the strike bug (wrong labels on ~31% of windows plus a t=0 `move_pct` tautology).
